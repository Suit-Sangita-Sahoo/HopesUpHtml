How To Deal With
A Toxic Friend

End a toxic friendship, protect your peace, and surround yourself with people who truly support you.

July 15, 2017 • The Hope Sup

Friends are an important part of our lives. As we grow up, our friends influence our thoughts, beliefs, and the way we see the world.

True friends care about us and support us without expecting something in return. Friendship, like every relationship, should be a two-way street.

But toxic friendships are different. Sometimes, without realizing it, we become friends with people who negatively affect our lives.

People change, and someone who once seemed like a great friend can become manipulative, negative, or emotionally draining.

My Experience With A Toxic Friend

I was in a toxic friendship for three years during high school. I was an introvert and cared too much about what other people thought of me.

I was also a people pleaser who found it difficult to say no. My friend, whom I'll call Z, was my classmate. After my best friend moved away, I felt lonely and started spending more time with Z.

At first, she seemed nice and popular. But eventually, I realized that she was manipulative and self-obsessed.

I wanted to belong to her group, so I allowed her to treat me badly because I was afraid of losing other friends too.

Later, I realized that the friendship was draining me. My grades were dropping, and I was starting to feel worthless.

Fortunately, I made some new friends who supported me. When school ended and I moved to another city for college, I finally had the chance to leave that friendship behind.

Sometimes, an unpleasant experience gives you the perspective and courage you need to move forward.

I reflected on my mistakes and became more confident. I learned to recognize true friends and understand which people I needed to keep at a safe distance.

You Know You Have A Toxic Friend When

1. They become jealous when you achieve something instead of being happy for you.

2. They dominate and manipulate you, making you misuse your time or resources for them.

3. They rarely encourage you, while you constantly praise and help them.

4. They never really listen to you.

5. They drain your positive energy and leave you feeling unhappy or insecure.

6. They appear to be a great friend to others, but you know their true behavior.

7. They speak negatively about your family while praising their own.

8. They constantly ask for favors but rarely appreciate your efforts or return them.

9. They make you feel lonely even when you are with them.

10. You feel powerless and angry with yourself for allowing the behavior.

You don't have to be nice to people who treat you badly. You don't have to like everyone.

You deserve friendships that make you feel respected, supported, and valued.

How To Get Rid Of A Toxic Friend

1. Avoid them. If possible, spend less time around them and stay close to people who make you feel good.

2. Spend more time with your true friends. Surround yourself with people who encourage and support you.

3. Focus on important things. Give more attention to your family, studies, career, and meaningful relationships.

4. Pick up a hobby. Try something productive such as playing a sport, learning an instrument, swimming, or simply doing something you enjoy.

5. Don't contact them. Gradually reduce communication and stop giving them the attention they use to control you.

6. Meet new people. Not everyone is toxic. There are good people who will encourage and support you.

Don't Blame Yourself

Never beat yourself up for making this mistake. It is natural to feel angry when you realize that you trusted the wrong person.

What matters is that you recognized the problem and made the decision to leave.

You have to forgive yourself and move forward.

See the experience as a life lesson. It can make you more aware, improve your self-esteem, and help you make better decisions in the future.

Not every friendship is meant to last forever. You should not waste your energy on people who constantly bring negativity into your life.

FREE YOURSELF

You don't have to stay in a friendship that makes you unhappy. Recognize the signs, protect your peace, and choose people who genuinely value you.
