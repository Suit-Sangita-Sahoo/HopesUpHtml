5 Things You Should
Never Do After An Argument

Arguments are inevitable, but how we respond afterward can make all the difference in our relationships.

July 18, 2026 • 6 min read

Arguments are inevitable. They can happen in any relationship, whether with parents, friends, co-workers, or a romantic partner.

How you react to and resolve arguments largely affects the dynamics of the relationship.

Arguing can leave us feeling overwhelmed and agitated. It's important to take some time to de-escalate things and cool off.

Avoid doing the following things when you're trying to regulate your emotions after an argument.

1. Hold Grudges

Holding grudges after an argument leads to more suffering and negatively affects your relationship.

Often, holding grudges is easier than forgiving the other person. However, holding grudges will escalate tension and build resentment in the long run.

Don't hold grudges or act in petty ways. Try to look beyond the flaws and negative attitude of the other person. Make the first move toward resolution.

By apologizing first, you let the other person know that you're admitting your wrongs and moving past the conflict.

“Apologizing doesn't make you weak. It shows humility and the maturity to own up to your behavior.”

An apology creates space for a meaningful discussion and helps both people work toward resolving the issues that caused the conflict.

2. Replay the Argument in Your Head

People are not always themselves when they are angry. They may say or do things that are out of character.

A lot of unpleasant things can be said during arguments. Constantly thinking about what the other person said only makes the situation feel worse.

By focusing too much on hurtful comments, you subject yourself to emotional pain again and again.

Free your mind from those comments. Avoid replaying the argument in your head or imagining different things you could have said.

REMEMBER

Give yourself time to cool off. Don't engage in negative thinking. Sometimes the best response is simply to pause and let go.

3. Bring Up Old Arguments

Many people bring up old arguments to get back at the other person. However, bringing up the past doesn't help the current situation.

Your goal should be to resolve the argument instead of prolonging it.

Bringing up past arguments can show that you haven't truly forgiven the other person for their past mistakes and are unable to move forward.

4. Make Big Decisions

When you're in a negative state of mind, your ability to make rational decisions is reduced.

It's better not to make important decisions right after a big argument. Wait until you're in a better mood and have more clarity.

When you're angry or upset, you're often thinking through the filter of your emotions.

“When emotions are running high, pause before making decisions you may later regret.”

Give yourself some time to calm down. Once your emotions settle, you'll be in a better position to weigh your options and exercise good judgment.

5. Jump to Conclusions

Having an intense argument doesn't automatically mean that the relationship is doomed.

We tend to exaggerate things said in the heat of the moment. Hurtful words may make you wonder if the relationship is going to end.

When we're angry, we're more likely to overreact and assume the worst-case scenario.

You can't truly know what the other person wanted to convey by considering only the things said during an argument.

Avoid jumping to the conclusion that the other person hates you based on things said during an emotional moment.

Give the other person some space. Spend some time alone reflecting on your thoughts and feelings. Take a few deep breaths and relax.

AFTER AN ARGUMENT

Pause.
Breathe. Reflect.

Whenever you are both ready, communicate honestly and try to sort things out calmly.

Remember that one argument does not define your entire relationship.

Choose calmness over reaction.

Arguments are difficult, but they don't have to destroy relationships. Giving yourself time to calm down can help you respond with greater understanding.

Instead of holding grudges, replaying arguments, bringing up the past, making big decisions, or jumping to conclusions, give yourself and the other person some space.

When you're both ready, communicate honestly and work toward understanding each other.
